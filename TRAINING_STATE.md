# CritiCall Training State

Last updated: 2026-09-10

## Exam and training approach

- Testing window: September 23–30, 2026.
- Practice rotation: no more than 4 consecutive items of one exercise type before rotating.
- Rotation should be highly randomized rather than predictable.
- Map reading is currently a low priority because it is a strength.
- Reading comprehension is strong, but harder questions involving exceptions, qualifiers, uncertainty, and rule overrides should continue.
- Numeric memory at 7 digits is currently strong.
- Spelling and high-accuracy typing remain important practice targets.

## Typing target

- 40 WPM or better
- 95% accuracy or better
- Five-minute blind transcription format

Current emphasis: protect accuracy first while maintaining speed above 40 WPM.

## Audio and data-entry preferences

- Use actual audio for audio drills.
- Present audio in short, naturally paced chunks with small pauses between fields.
- Data-entry responses are entered in ALL CAPS.
- Licence-plate spacing after the first three characters is useful formatting but should not be treated as an error when all characters are correct.
- Every name is repeated 5 times after entry.
- Difficult names and streets should receive extra reinforcement.

Known reinforcement targets: KILPATRICK, MCPHEE, MENZIES, CLIFFE, HEADQUARTERS, ANDERTON, PRITCHARD.

## Numeric memory

- Maximum sequence length: 7 digits.
- Use audio presentation.
- Recent 7-digit performance has been strong.

## Spelling

Audio spelling is preferred in chat: say the word without displaying its spelling first, then have the user type it.

Recurring targets include surveillance, occurrence, questionnaire, accommodate, privilege, embarrassment, maintenance, personnel, necessary, immediately, separate, harassment, suspicious, recommendation, and possession.

After a spelling item, reinforce the correct spelling with 5 typed repetitions.

## Decision Making

Use the CritiCall practice classification rules rather than substituting general real-world dispatch assumptions.

- Police: attempted/threatened physical harm, actual physical harm, or harm to another person's property.
- Fire: immediate signs of fire such as flames or smoke, a fire alarm, or a trapped/confined person needing rescue or release.
- EMS: an emergency medical condition requiring medically trained intervention.
- Public Utility: malfunctioning/broken public water, electrical power systems (including power lines, streetlights and traffic signals), natural-gas systems used for home heating, or blocked sewer drainpipes.

Important: under these practice classifications, a natural-gas-system problem maps to **Utility**.

## Call summarization

Emphasize:

LOCATION → SERVICE → WHAT → DANGER → INJURIES → WEAPONS → SUSPECT → UPDATES

Keep observed facts separate from caller assumptions. Preserve uncertainty and conflicting information rather than resolving it without evidence.
